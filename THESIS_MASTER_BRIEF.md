### 1.1 The problem domain
Dense neural SLAM for **deformable endoscopic (minimally-invasive) surgery**. Unlike rigid-scene SLAM, several motion sources occur simultaneously and must be disentangled from a single moving (monocular/stereo) endoscope feed:
1. **Endoscope ego-motion** — the camera itself moves (the only thing classical rigid SLAM models).
2. **Non-rigid tissue deformation** — heartbeat, breathing, peristalsis, and tool–tissue contact continuously warp the scene; the "map" is genuinely time-varying.
3. **An independently moving rigid tool** — a surgical instrument enters/leaves frame and moves on its own SE(3) trajectory, decoupled from both camera and tissue.

The system must **jointly** estimate (a) the camera trajectory and (b) a time-varying 3D scene representation. The substrate is **DDS-SLAM** — our fork of IRMVLab/DDS-SLAM (pinned at commit `009977b`), itself a fork of **Co-SLAM** (hash-grid neural-SDF SLAM). DDS-SLAM adds to Co-SLAM essentially two author contributions on top of the Co-SLAM map/renderer/tracker: a **deformation field** (`TimeNet`, which warps *where* the map is sampled via `inputs_flat = pts + vox_motion`, reported at `scene_rep.py:201`) and a **semantic-distance head/loss (SDL)** [VERIFIED — DDS-SLAM at matched config is byte-identical to Co-SLAM upstream; deformation field + SDL head are the only two author additions; `DDS-SLAM-Base/` is the pristine reference].

### 1.2 The three original failure modes (motivation)
*Writer note: the F-A/F-B/F-C labels are a synthesis framing imposed for this thesis, not source-labelled categories; the underlying mechanisms below are source-supported.*
- **F-A: fast tool motion** — a rapidly moving instrument corrupts tracking/mapping (no per-object motion model; the tool mask collapses to an *edge* field, the FM1 "tool-masks-are-edges" failure) [VERIFIED — FM1 catalogued].
- **F-B: large deformation mistaken for camera motion** — tissue warp leaks into the pose estimate, because pose is optimised against a **scalar reduced loss with no per-ray weight/mask on the pose residual** (reported at `ddsslam.py:571`); `def_reg` penalises ‖Δx‖² and the field gates Δx into the *map*, not into pose [VERIFIED in source].
- **F-C: canonical scene never updating** — the static canonical map does not adapt as tissue deforms or as the tool sweeps through, producing ghosts/smear. This "no-adaptation → ghosts" behaviour is the live problem statement for the v1/v2 map-adaptation work [VERIFIED — observed in base characterisation].

## 2. Background — NeRF, Neural-SDF, Co-SLAM, Deformation Fields, 3DGS

Technical primer for the thesis writer. Sections (a)–(c), (e-head), (f-head) are ESTABLISHED literature background and are safe to assert. ### (a) Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [Mildenhall et al., ECCV 2020]
A NeRF is a coordinate MLP F_Θ: (**x**, **d**) → (**c**, σ), mapping a 3D position **x**=(x,y,z) and a viewing direction **d** to an emitted RGB colour **c** and a volume density σ. A pixel's colour is rendered by quadrature of the volume-rendering integral along the camera ray **r**(t)=**o**+t**d**. The discretised estimator over N samples with inter-sample distances δ_i = t_{i+1}−t_i is:

  **C(r) = Σ_i T_i · (1 − exp(−σ_i δ_i)) · c_i,  where  T_i = exp(−Σ_{j<i} σ_j δ_j)**

T_i is accumulated transmittance (probability the ray reaches sample i un-occluded); (1−exp(−σ_i δ_i)) is the per-sample opacity (alpha). Training is purely **photometric** — an L2 loss between rendered and observed pixel colours over posed images; **no explicit 3D supervision** is required. [VERIFIED — established]. Because an MLP cannot natively fit high spatial frequencies (spectral bias), NeRF applies **positional / Fourier encoding** γ(p) = (sin(2⁰πp), cos(2⁰πp), …, sin(2^{L−1}πp), cos(2^{L−1}πp)) to inputs before the MLP, enabling sharp geometry/texture. Cost: per-pixel ray-marching through a deep MLP → minutes-to-hours per scene, far from SLAM-rate. [VERIFIED — established]

### (b) Instant-NGP multiresolution hash encoding [Müller et al., ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH 2022]
Replaces (most of) the deep MLP with a **multiresolution hash table** of trainable feature vectors. Space is covered by L grid levels at geometrically-spaced resolutions; each level's voxel corners index a fixed-size hash table; the looked-up features are trilinearly interpolated, concatenated across levels, then decoded by a small MLP. This (i) moves capacity from MLP weights into O(1) hash lookups, (ii) lets gradients touch only the few features a ray actually hits, and (iii) is implemented in fused CUDA (tinycudann). Net effect: large speedup (orders of magnitude over a plain deep-MLP NeRF) → **this is what makes neural-field SLAM run at interactive/near-real-time rates**, and it is the encoding backbone Co-SLAM (and thus DDS-SLAM) builds on. [VERIFIED — established]. (Implementation note for repro, OUR stack: tinycudann must be rebuilt for the live GPU SM arch — sm_75 T4 / sm_80 A100 — and TF32 forced OFF for the precision-sensitive SDF/pose path. [VERIFIED — workspace ops])

### (c) Neural-SDF variant [NeuS — Wang et al., NeurIPS 2021; VolSDF — Yariv et al., NeurIPS 2021]
Instead of an opaque density field, the geometry MLP outputs a **signed distance function** s(**x**), whose **zero-level-set {x : s(x)=0} is the surface**. For rendering, the SDF is mapped to a density/opacity via a bell-shaped transform centred on the surface — NeuS uses a logistic-sigmoid (s-density) formulation, VolSDF an unbiased Laplacian-CDF transform — so the same volume-rendering quadrature in (a) still applies. SLAM systems use a **truncation-based** loss family: within a band ±tr of the surface an **SDF/truncation loss** drives s toward the observed depth's signed distance, and a **free-space loss** forces s≈tr in front of the surface (empty space). **Why SDF over raw density for SLAM:** (i) a directly **extractable surface** (marching cubes on the zero level-set) for mapping/meshing; (ii) **depth-supervised, metrically-grounded geometry** rather than a fuzzy density cloud; (iii) better-conditioned pose optimisation. [VERIFIED — established]. (OUR SLAM ingests MoGe-2 monocular depth which is **up-to-scale**, so these truncation constants act partly as pose regularisers and must NOT be naively rescaled to the scene. [PARKED/NEGATIVE — tuning note: scaling trunc/range_d to scene WORSENED ATE in our runs])

### (d) Co-SLAM [Wang, Bruns, Torr, Prisacariu — CVPR 2023] — the substrate DDS-SLAM forks
Joint **coordinate-MLP neural-SLAM** system. Key design: a **hybrid encoding** = Instant-NGP multiresolution **hash grid** (high-frequency detail, fast convergence) concatenated with **one-blob / coordinate encoding** (smooth, hole-filling, coherence) feeding a small geometry+colour MLP with an SDF head. It performs **joint mapping and camera-pose (tracking) optimisation** under photometric + depth + SDF/free-space losses, with **global bundle adjustment over a keyframe set** (jointly refining pose + map across sampled keyframe rays). [VERIFIED — established].

**DDS-SLAM is a fork of Co-SLAM** [DDS-SLAM, IROS 2024]. OUR adversarial audit (workflow wlzja28ry, 2026-06-12) found map/renderer/tracker (3D hash-grid SDF, sdf2weights, const-velocity tracking, global BA) **byte-identical to Co-SLAM** (`DDS-SLAM-Base/` pristine == IRMVLab @ commit `009977b`; Co-SLAM source contains ZERO edge/time/semantic code). DDS-SLAM adds only two authors'-original components: (1) a **TimeNet 4D deformation field** (paper's #1 headline) and (2) a **semantic distance-field loss** (paper's "SDL", code `EdgeNet_Semantic`/`edge_semantic`). [VERIFIED — established that these are the deltas].

### (e) Deformation fields [D-NeRF — Pumarola et al., CVPR 2021; Nerfies — Park et al., ICCV 2021]
For non-rigid/dynamic scenes, a **canonical-plus-warp** construction: a deformation MLP D(**x**, t) → Δ**x** maps an observed point at time t back into a static **canonical** field, which is then rendered as in (a). [VERIFIED — established]. DDS-SLAM's **TimeNet** is exactly this — the warp is applied at a single chokepoint, `inputs_flat = pts + vox_motion` (`model/scene_rep.py:239`), with a t=0 ⇒ Δx=0 anchor (`:218`). [VERIFIED — code-traced].

**Critical background for the FIX arm:** D-NeRF / Nerfies stabilise this ill-posed warp with regularisers — Nerfies pins known-static regions using static SfM/COLMAP points (background regularisation) plus elastic/ARAP-style priors. On **deforming endoscopic tissue these static SfM points are NOT available** (no reliable static structure; sub-SNR motion), so that regulariser cannot be ported. [VERIFIED — established + dataset-grounded].

OUR paper-vs-code audit found DDS-SLAM's field is **starved by design and faithful to its paper**: it uses only render-path losses, with NONE acting directly on Δx, and the paper deliberately forgoes correspondence/flow priors. In OUR reproduced checkpoints the field is effectively inert (Δx ≡ 0.0, bit-exact across all 311 frames), so DDS-SLAM as-run ≈ static Co-SLAM + a low-weight semantic-distance head. [VERIFIED — but checkpoint-scoped: inertness is established on our runs, NOT on the authors' released checkpoints]. ### 3.1 What DDS-SLAM is
DDS-SLAM is a **neural-SDF dense RGB-D SLAM** system aimed at **deformable endoscopic surgery**. Object of study = the fork **IRMVLab/DDS-SLAM @ `009977b`** (mirrored pristine in `DDS-SLAM-Base/`, our working copy in `DDS-SLAM/`). **[VERIFIED]** DDS-SLAM is a **Co-SLAM fork**: the map representation, volume renderer, and camera tracker are ~byte-identical to upstream Co-SLAM — upstream Co-SLAM source carries **ZERO** edge/time/semantic code; the only such hits in our tree are *our own ported diagnostics*. ### 3.2 The SLAM loop (cite `ddsslam.py`)
Driver `DDSSLAM.run()` (`:1107`) iterates a `DataLoader`:
- **First frame** → `first_frame_mapping()` (`:305`): `first_iters` map steps, pose pinned to GT/first; always a keyframe.
- **Every later frame** → `tracking_render()` (`:768`): per-frame **pose optimisation with the map frozen**. Inits via constant-velocity `predict_current_pose()` (`:714`), optimises a per-frame `(cur_rot, cur_trans)` with Adam (`lr_rot`/`lr_trans`) over `tracking.iter` iterations, keeps the **best-loss** pose (`tracking.best`). Loss = photometric + depth + SDF/free-space (+ semantic-distance). **[VERIFIED]** (function locations confirmed).
- **Mapping** runs on `i % map_every == 0`: `global_BA()` (`:539`) over a keyframe buffer + the current frame — the **dominant trainer** (`mapping.iters` steps/frame), optimising the hash grids, decoders, AND keyframe poses (the first pose stays fixed) — followed by `current_frame_mapping()` (`:390`, `cur_frame_iters` steps, the per-frame sharpening pass; **early-returns when `cur_frame_iters<=0`**). **[DESIGNED]** (BA-internal optimiser scope described from code structure, not re-measured).

## 4. Methodology & Evaluation Framework

This thesis is governed by one rule and one experimental loop. The writer must present these as the epistemic foundation that makes every result in later sections credible: the metrics are chosen and reported in a way that is specifically robust to the failure modes of monocular, up-to-scale, sub-SNR surgical sequences.

**(a) The METRIC-FIRST governing rule [VERIFIED — locked user policy, 2026-06-15].** Metrics are the *only* arbiter of success: render quality (PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS) and tracking accuracy (Sim3-corrected ATE). `DDS-SLAM-Base/` (pristine IRMVLab DDS-SLAM @ commit `009977b`) is the untouched, eternal reference against which every change is diffed and judged. Crucially, deformation-field diagnostics (liveness / gradient / `field3d` / time-convention probes) were **demoted** from a success criterion to a "what is it doing" explanatory tool — they *explain* why a change helped, they do **not** ship. The user's exact framing: "All our testing on the deformation field isn't improving results — it's just testing what the field is doing." A change that revives the field but does not move PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS or ATE is **not** counted as an improvement. (Note: "pristine model behaviour" is scoped to *default behaviour* — diagnostics, hooks, eval, viz, configs, the MoGe-2 `depth_subdir` loader, and inert-by-default observation hooks such as `deformation_off` are all permitted in base because they do not change the default forward path; only behaviour-altering changes are FIX/IMPROVE.)

**(b) The TWO-BRANCH structure + the isolation loop [VERIFIED — policy].** Work is split into two branches kept strictly separate until a final COMBINE: **FIX** (revive the inert deformation field by changing existing code only — `global_BA`/time-convention) and **IMPROVE** (add metric-winning *features* built FROM the pristine base with NONE of the FIX changes: Inc-1 aleatoric σ² head, Inc-2 tracking pose down-weight, WildGS/DINO, SNI, NRGS). The loop is: **one change → run it FROM the base in isolation (not stacked) → did OUR metric improve? → yes → keep (merge to the improving line) → no → drop.** Rationale for separation: do not build features on unconfirmed fixes (an earlier `time_normalize` "fix" was later found to have silently introduced a bug). COMBINE is last and answers "did fixing add value *on top of* improving?" by comparing IMPROVE-only vs (FIX+IMPROVE), which attributes the fix's contribution. The port to new data is **infrastructure-only** (MoGe-2 depth loading, eval, inline-render video wrapped *around* the pristine model); model logic stays pristine by default. Flag-gating supports the loop — each change is a default-off flag, A/B'd off-vs-on from base.

**(c) The Sim3-ATE metric — `Addons/eval/sim3_ate.py` [VERIFIED — code-read; commit `77cb4a1`].** This is the canonical CRCD tracking metric. It applies **Umeyama alignment WITH scale** (Sim3, `umeyama(..., with_scale=True)`), reporting: Sim3 ATE (rmse/mean/median/max in mm), **recovered scale `s`** (printed as `est ~1/s × GT metric scale`), **est/GT path-length ratio** (computed *after* applying `s`), and **dominant-axis |Pearson|** (scale-free shape tracking; the dominant axis is selected as the largest GT extent, `argmax(g.max−g.min)`). It also prints the rigid ATE for contrast. **Why the pipeline's own `output.txt` is WRONG:** `tools/eval_ate.py:align()` (the rigid SVD, ~L67–101) is **rigid Horn with NO scale**, and `pose_evaluation(..., scale, ...)` is invoked with `scale=1`. On up-to-scale monocular MoGe-2 depth the est trajectory is ~7.5–8× the GT metric scale, so the rigid ATE is *dominated by the global scale mismatch*, not tracking quality — on CRCD C1_001 it reads ~38–48 mm (DebugLogger box variant ~60–67 mm) and even **INVERTS the A/B**, reporting +uncertainty as +27% WORSE when the scale-corrected truth is the opposite. **Never headline `output.txt`.** This matters because **neural SLAM is NOT scale-invariant**: unlike classical reprojection-error SLAM (ORB-SLAM3/COLMAP), the loss here is volumetric rendering + SDF in *metric* space, shaped by hardcoded metric-distance constants (`bound`, `trunc`=0.1 m, `range_d`=0.05 m, `voxel_sdf`=0.001 m, `near`/`far`, `lr_trans`). Wrong input scale → different optimization landscape → worse local minimum that Sim3 alignment cannot recover. Empirical proof [VERIFIED — single controlled pair, n=1]: same data/alignment, depth ×1.0 → Sim3 ATE 3.68 mm vs depth ×0.30 → Sim3 ATE 2.51 mm (~32% better) — a per-frame closeness delta that Sim3 alone cannot synthesise (it can only globally rescale/rotate/translate). So "Sim3 at eval covers scale" is true for classical feature-based SLAM, **false here**; input depth must be pre-rescaled per snippet.

**(d) The sub-SNR rule [VERIFIED — canonical].** ALL CRCD snippets sit below the tracker precision floor. The floor is ~1 mm/frame as the post-`global_BA` blended pose step (set by `lr_trans=1e-3 × iters≈10` with Adam dynamics; the tracker stage *alone* is ~2.1 mm/f at default lr — the ~1 mm figure is the blend). GT per-frame motion is far smaller: ~9–69 μm/frame across snippets (C_1 all-frame avg 0.069 mm/f ≈ 69 μm, active-frame median ~0.115 mm/f; F_1 ~12 μm/f; F_3 ~9 μm/f) — i.e. ~5–15× below floor by all-frame measure (and still ~8.7× below floor even at C_1's active-frame median). Consequence: Sim3 ATE *alone* is misleading because Sim3 alignment places the est cluster centroid on the GT centroid, so the residual measures **cluster spread, not tracker fidelity** — a "sit still" tracker scores *better* on sub-SNR data. Detection (the smoking gun): SM_v3 reported a tight 2.22 mm Sim3 ATE but est/GT **path-ratio was 33×** (est 607 mm vs GT 18.55 mm) — a jittery hover, not tracking. **Always quote the triple: Sim3 ATE + est/GT path-ratio + dominant-axis |Pearson|.** A config "actually tracks" only when path-ratio → ~1 (≤1.5–2× acceptable), Sim3 scale → ~1, and per-axis Pearson > ~0.5. Historic context: all 9 logged DDS-SLAM/Co-SLAM runs had |Pearson| < 0.2 (the decoupled regime; the fingerprint is shared across both codebases, i.e. upstream of their code differences). The canon C1_001 +uncertainty result lifts path-ratio 7.0→4.1 (toward 1) and |Pearson|dom 0.81→0.97 (well above the <0.2 regime) — real shape-tracking gains on the trackable (dominant) axis — but path-ratio is still ~4×, so this is NOT "tracks perfectly," only a real *relative* improvement on the shape-aware metrics.

**(e) n=3 seeds for a verdict [VERIFIED — standing rule].** Seed coin-flip is real; the base seed-std is the noise floor a claimed win must clear (non-overlapping across n=3). The canon CRCD C1_001 +uncertainty result — Sim3 ATE_mean 3.15→2.43 mm (−23%), ATE_max 14.79→9.43 mm (−36%) — is **[VERIFIED n=3, non-overlapping]**.

**(f) Parity gating — `Addons/regression/test_inc0_bitidentical.py` [VERIFIED — code-read].** Every feature is flag-gated default-off so the fork @ all-flags-off is bit-identical to base. The Inc-0 gate proves this via the **torch+CUDA RNG state captured immediately AFTER `JointEncoding` construction** (a leaked module's `nn.Linear`/tcnn weight-init would draw RNG and diverge the state), plus **parameter count (`n_params`), parameter-tensor count (`n_param_tensors`), and the `state_dict` key set**. It also sanity-checks that the flags-ON build has ≥ as many params as flags-off. RNG-after-build is used (not an `est_c2w` byte-cmp) because `seed_everything` does not set `cudnn.deterministic`/`use_deterministic_algorithms` and tinycudann HashGrid atomic-add kernels are non-bit-reproducible on GPU — so a byte-cmp is flaky even base-vs-base. Flags checked: `uncertainty`, `nrgs`, `sni`.

**(g) Two diagnostic sets per run [VERIFIED — standing rule, 2026-06-18].** Every run ships BOTH, by default (never bolted on after): **NUMERICAL** (the rigorous arbiter — the Sim3 triple [Sim3 ATE + est/GT path-ratio + dominant-axis |Pearson|], PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, plus per-task diagnostics: field |Δx|/reduction/cos/anchor; teacher-gate reduction-by-magnitude/cycle/Lowe-ratio; uncertainty outlier-σ² fraction in low-gradient regions) AND **VISUAL** (how the user reasons — the canonical **6-panel inline video** via `Addons/viz/generate_video.py`, plus σ²/depth/render/PCA-feature panels). Rationale: a metric can hide what a video shows (render PSNR ≈ base while σ² visibly sits on the pins and softens depth around deformation) and a video cannot *prove* a win — neither alone suffices. Videos are **inline in-training renders only** (`render_all_frames.py` deleted — post-hoc quality drop). Visuals **auto-ship** co-located with each payload (`MyDrive/Outputs/manual_cells/<name>/figs/` for `run_cell.sh`; inside the run dir / `payload.tgz` for the `a100_*.sh` runbooks). Scope = GPU runs + the pin-EPE judge ONLY; cheap pure-numpy bakes/validators stay text-only.

**(h) Shared-box isolation traps [VERIFIED — hard-won, 2026-06-18].** When two agents share one Colab box + the `/content/DDS-SLAM` tree: (1) runbooks hardcoding `REPO=/content/DDS-SLAM` mean a separate clone does NOT isolate — fix is self-location `REPO=$(cd "$(dirname "$0")/../.." && pwd)`; (2) **staging skips on existence, not completeness** (a 213-frame, no-depth partial sync → `IndexError` at `dataset.py:169` — verify depth-count == frame-count before skipping, or stage to an isolated dir); (3) DINO bake skips on file *count* not *format* → wrong-shape grids load silently; (4) `git reset --hard origin/<branch>` as a clone-fallback can wipe another agent's uncommitted tree — use `git fetch && git checkout`/`git pull`, never `--hard` on a shared box; (5) "use left frames" means EVERYTHING (SLAM staging + MoGe + intrinsics), not just depth-gen; (6) **eval-OOM silently produces 0-byte metrics** (LPIPS SIGKILL on a contended GPU + block-buffered stdout → run `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES="" python -u Addons/eval/eval_rendering.py` for CPU LPIPS, unbuffered, so a kill cannot swallow output). Operational gates: the `.DONE`-only-on-success convention (`.FAILED` on crash) prevents crashed cells being skipped on re-run; and **use `deformation_off:True` (not the broken `dynamic:False`)** for field-off ablations — `dynamic:False` leaves the 4-col time-augmented ray input unstripped → a `scene_rep.py` bounding-box 4-vs-3 crash.
