## SUMMARY
Alpha-blended depth from the stock Luiten rasterizer is adequate for TRACKING in low-parallax surgical SLAM but is the known weak point for depth accuracy: two 2025 systems (Endo-2DTAM/ICRA-2025 on C3VD, GauS-SLAM) document its two specific biases — depth underestimation wherever accumulated alpha < 1, and background-Gaussian contamination of foreground depth — and both fix most of it cheaply by weight-normalizing the blended depth (D = sum(w_i z_i)/sum(w_i)), before any rasterizer swap. The measured ceiling for a full 2DGS-surfel rasterizer swap in an EndoGSLAM-style loop is calibrated by Endo-2DTAM: depth RMSE 2.17 -> 1.87 mm (-14%) vs EndoGSLAM-H, ATE unchanged (0.34 -> 0.33 mm), PSNR DROPS ~2 dB, tracking 15% slower — and its code (SplaTAM-style loop + hbb1 diff-surfel-rasterization, EndoGSLAM's own C3VD data) is a near-direct integration template if we want it. RaDe-GS gives the best geometry while keeping 3D Gaussians (DTU 0.68 mm, ~Neuralangelo) but its rasterizer stack (anisotropic 3DGS + GOF densification) is far from SplaTAM's isotropic setup, so integration cost is high; PGSR's unbiased plane-distance depth is the formulation surgical offline work (EndoPlanar, MICCAI 2025) already lifted with large depth-RMSE gains. For monocular-depth supervision, best practice is per-image/per-snippet scale-shift anchoring + gradient-aware or reliability-masked L1 (DN-Splatter, In-Depth-We-Trust CVPR26-WS); global Pearson losses destroy the metric anchor we need for depth-L1 reporting, and MoDGS shows per-FRAME scale inconsistency of mono depth in dynamic scenes breaks even Pearson — its static-region least-squares per-frame rescale + ordinal loss is the relevant fix for our P2. For a reconstruction claim, the standard is TSDF-fusing rendered depth at estimated poses (2DGS, RaDe-GS, GauS-SLAM all do this; GOF's direct extraction is offline-only) — but note our depth-L1 against MoGe-2 is circular (eval prior = training prior), so a mesh/depth claim needs an independent reference (stereo depth on CRCD, C3VD-style GT, or STIR). Mip-Splatting matters only when the sampling rate changes (focal/working-distance zoom); at fixed endoscope intrinsics and near-constant standoff it is low EV. Ranked EV: (1) alpha-normalized depth + silhouette gating = near-zero cost, (2) per-frame scale rectification of MoGe-2 + reliability-masked depth L1, (3) TSDF-fused mesh for the reconstruction claim, (4) optional diff-surfel-rasterization swap via the Endo-2DTAM template only if the depth/reconstruction claim becomes central, (5) RaDe-GS/GOF and Mip-Splatting not worth the integration cost in our loop.

## SUMMARY
The field has converged on four time representations for online dynamic GS: (1) per-frame increments with stored per-frame state (Luiten Dynamic3DGS, 3DGStream NTC, QUEEN residuals, IGS/ComGS with keyframe error-correction); (2) time-LOCALIZED basis functions in absolute time with partial activation + replay (Free-DyGS, NRGS-SLAM, SE(3) B-splines, Spacetime-Gaussians/SwinGS temporal-extent primitives); (3) global time-conditioned MLP kept alive by historical-keyframe replay (4DTAM, CVPR 2025, the only released-code online non-rigid GS SLAM); (4) keyframe-anchored explicit states with interpolation (Ex4DGS, ComGS keypoints). CRITICAL: our crux is already solved in the surgical literature — Free-DyGS (arXiv 2409.01003) runs Deform3DGS's exact learnable-Gaussian-RBF basis frame-by-frame with pose estimation, by seeding basis centers over absolute frame-index time, optimizing only the bases whose centers are near the current timestamp ("partial activation"), and replaying a trailing window (Retrospective Deformation Recapitulation, w=100) to stop sequential training from overwriting historical deformations; NRGS-SLAM (Feb 2026) does the same per-Gaussian temporal-Gaussian-basis trick inside a full monocular endoscopic GS-SLAM with per-frame residual updates on basis weights and a learned per-Gaussian deformation probability that down-weights tracking — a direct novelty threat to our P1+P2 combination (neither has released code yet). RANKING for our loop: #1 time-localized per-Gaussian RBF basis in absolute time + partial activation + retrospective replay (keeps Deform3DGS machinery intact, our dx* teacher gives it the direct supervision that render-only training lacks, past-time deformation stays evaluable so held-state re-render works, old bases freeze = no forgetting; cost: replay needed because the canonical map keeps evolving — exactly our BUILD-LAW gauge lesson); #2 keyframe-anchored explicit displacement states + Hermite/slerp interpolation (Ex4DGS pattern; trivially supervisable by baked dx*, exact held-state at keyframes, rides EndoGSLAM's existing keyframe machinery; cost: misses fast inter-keyframe deformation and published versions are offline/multi-view); #3 per-frame increments with stored states (3DGStream/QUEEN; zero forgetting by construction and no time parameterization at all, but storage grows per frame, snapshots go stale when later BA moves the map, and error accumulation demands keyframe refits — the whole streaming-FVV literature (IGS, ComGS) exists to patch this). The global-MLP+replay route (4DTAM) is proven online but is architecturally the same render-loss-trained global time MLP that died in DDS-SLAM, so we rank it below all three. Every streaming paper that survives past ~300 frames pairs its motion model with an explicit anti-drift mechanism (keyframe refit or replay) — we should treat that as mandatory, and our held-state metric is the right judge because per-frame-recency eval is exactly what let error accumulation hide in early streaming work.
