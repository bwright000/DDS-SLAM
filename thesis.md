## 1. NeRF — How It Works

### 1. Core idea: the scene as a continuous radiance field
A Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) represents a static 3D scene implicitly as a single coordinate-based MLP, $F_\Theta : (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$, mapping a 3D position $\mathbf{x}=(x,y,z)$ and a viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ (expressed as a 3D Cartesian unit vector $(d_x,d_y,d_z)$, i.e. the 2-DoF direction $(\theta,\phi)$) to an emitted RGB colour $\mathbf{c}$ and a volume density $\sigma$ (Mildenhall et al. 2020). Density is predicted from position only (so geometry is forced to be multi-view consistent); colour is conditioned additionally on view direction to model non-Lambertian / view-dependent effects. The scene is thus stored entirely in the network weights $\Theta$ rather than in an explicit mesh, voxel grid, or point cloud.

### 2. Volume rendering
A pixel is rendered by casting the camera ray $\mathbf{r}(t)=\mathbf{o}+t\mathbf{d}$, sampling $N$ ordered points along it between near and far bounds, querying $F_\Theta$ at each, and alpha-compositing the returned colours weighted by per-sample opacity and accumulated transmittance. The discretised emission–absorption (numerical-quadrature) form of the volume rendering integral is

$$\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i\,\bigl(1-\exp(-\sigma_i\,\delta_i)\bigr)\,\mathbf{c}_i, \qquad T_i = \exp\!\Bigl(-\sum_{j<i}\sigma_j\,\delta_j\Bigr),$$

where $\delta_i = t_{i+1}-t_i$ is the inter-sample distance, $\alpha_i = 1-\exp(-\sigma_i\delta_i)$ is the sample opacity, and $T_i$ is the accumulated transmittance (the fraction of light reaching sample $i$ unoccluded). This rendering operator is fully differentiable, so gradients flow from pixel colours back to $\Theta$.

### 3. Positional / Fourier encoding
A plain MLP exhibits spectral bias toward low frequencies and fits blurry geometry. NeRF therefore maps each scalar input coordinate (positions normalised to $[-1,1]$, plus the direction components) through a fixed sinusoidal Fourier feature encoding

$$\gamma(p) = \bigl(\sin(2^0\pi p), \cos(2^0\pi p), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1}\pi p), \cos(2^{L-1}\pi p)\bigr)$$

before the network, lifting inputs to a higher-dimensional space and enabling the MLP to represent high-frequency texture and sharp detail (Mildenhall et al. 2020). The original work uses $L=10$ frequency bands for position and $L=4$ for direction.

### 4. Training
NeRF is optimised purely from a set of posed RGB images by minimising the squared photometric error between rendered and observed pixels, $\mathcal{L} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}} \lVert \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C_{\text{gt}}(\mathbf{r}) \rVert_2^2$. There is no explicit 3D supervision; geometry emerges implicitly from enforcing multi-view photometric consistency. The original NeRF trains two networks (coarse and fine) jointly: the coarse pass produces a piecewise-constant density estimate from which a second set of samples is drawn by inverse-transform importance sampling, concentrating the fine network's samples in high-density (near-surface) regions to improve efficiency. (RGB-D variants such as the SLAM systems below additionally add a depth term.)

### 5. Hash-grid acceleration (Instant-NGP)
The original NeRF needs hours to days because a large MLP is queried millions of times. Instant-NGP (Müller et al. 2022) replaces most of that MLP capacity with a **multiresolution hash encoding**: learnable feature vectors stored across $L$ resolution levels, each a fixed-size hash table indexed from the surrounding grid-cell corners; features are (tri)linearly interpolated at the query point, concatenated across levels, and decoded by a very small MLP. Hash collisions are not resolved explicitly but are disambiguated implicitly by the multiresolution structure and optimisation. This compresses training from hours to seconds while preserving quality, and is the backbone that makes neural representations practical for real-time SLAM.

### 6. The neural-SDF variant
For cleaner, extractable geometry, the volume density is replaced by a **signed-distance function** (SDF) $s(\mathbf{x})$ whose zero level-set is the surface. A fixed mapping converts the SDF to a density-like opacity for the same volume-rendering integral: VolSDF expresses density as a Laplace cumulative-distribution function of the SDF (Yariv et al. 2021), while NeuS derives a logistic-sigmoid (S-density) weighting that is unbiased (to first order) and occlusion-aware (Wang et al. 2021). These yield well-defined surfaces and consistent normals while keeping rendering differentiable.

**Co-SLAM** (Wang et al. 2023) — and DDS-SLAM as its fork — sits in this neural-SDF/RGB-D SLAM lineage but uses a lighter, truncated-SDF formulation rather than the NeuS/VolSDF density transforms. Its distinctive design is a **joint coordinate + sparse-parametric encoding**: a multiresolution hash grid (for fast convergence and high-frequency detail) combined with one-blob coordinate encoding (for surface coherence and hole-filling in unobserved regions), feeding small SDF and colour decoders. Supervision is photometric loss plus **truncation-based SDF losses** — free-space and near-surface (signed-distance) terms within a truncation band around the observed depth — together with a depth term; Co-SLAM further performs global bundle adjustment over all keyframes.

### 7. Why SDF over raw density for SLAM
An SDF gives a directly **extractable surface** (zero level-set, e.g. via marching cubes) and a metric notion of distance-to-surface, where raw density does not. Combined with depth supervision this yields sharper, better-constrained depth rendering, depth-supervised tracking, and stable geometry for mapping and loop closure — the geometric grounding that SLAM requires and on which DDS-SLAM's deformable extension is layered.

**References:** Mildenhall et al. 2020 (NeRF); Müller et al. 2022 (Instant-NGP); Wang et al. 2021 (NeuS); Yariv et al. 2021 (VolSDF); Wang et al. 2023 (Co-SLAM, CVPR 2023 / arXiv:2304.14377).

## 2. DDS-SLAM — What It Is, How It Works, Why Chosen

### What it is

DDS-SLAM (IRMVLab) is a dense, neural-implicit RGB-D SLAM system built for **deformable endoscopic surgery**. It is a **fork of Co-SLAM** (Wang et al., CVPR 2023), a real-time coordinate-network SLAM that represents the scene as a neural signed-distance function (SDF) and jointly optimises that map together with the camera trajectory. Co-SLAM's central trick is a **hybrid encoding** — a multiresolution hash grid (for fast, high-frequency detail) feeding small MLP decoders — which DDS-SLAM inherits essentially unchanged (the map, renderer, and tracker are byte-identical to upstream Co-SLAM).

On top of the Co-SLAM substrate, DDS-SLAM adds **two** author contributions:
1. A **time-conditioned deformation field** (the "TimeNet" MLP). Where Co-SLAM assumes a rigid, static scene observed by a moving camera, DDS-SLAM learns a *canonical* SDF scene plus a warp `D(x, t)` that displaces each query point to its position at timestamp `t`. Conceptually this follows the canonical-plus-warp construction of D-NeRF/Nerfies (the paper cites D-NeRF for the *construction* only), letting non-rigid tissue (breathing, tool–tissue contact) be modelled as deformations of one shared canonical reconstruction.
2. A **semantic distance field / loss** (an extra render-supervised head). This is alive but minor and strongly dataset-dependent (sizeable loss share on the authors' surgical data, near-inert ~0.001% on StereoMIS), so it cannot by itself explain a tracking advantage.

Our working copy forks **IRMVLab/DDS-SLAM @ `009977b`**.

### How it works

The system runs the standard neural-SLAM loop, frame by frame (verified against `ddsslam.py:run`):

- **Representation.** A hash grid encodes position; small decoders map the encoding to an SDF value and an RGB colour. Geometry is supervised through volumetric rendering with **truncation-based losses** — a free-space loss (`fs_loss`) that drives the SDF toward truncation away from surfaces and an SDF/truncation loss (`sdf_loss`) near the surface (`get_sdf_loss`, `scene_rep.py`) — alongside photometric (colour) and depth render losses, plus the semantic-distance term.
- **Tracking.** For each incoming frame the map is frozen and the **camera pose** is optimised by minimising the rendered-vs-observed photometric, depth, and SDF losses over sampled rays (`tracking_render`).
- **Mapping.** Selected keyframes enter a buffer; on a `map_every` cadence the system performs **`global_BA`** over the keyframe buffer (the dominant map trainer) plus **`current_frame_mapping`**, jointly refining the scene network and the buffered poses (`ddsslam.py:1142-1144`).
- **Depth.** For our experiments depth comes from the **MoGe-2 monocular** model. This is *up-to-scale*, so each snippet is **stereo-anchored** to recover a metric scale before it conditions the SDF. (This is our infrastructure choice for CRCD/SemSup, not an intrinsic part of upstream DDS-SLAM, which consumes RGB-D.)
- **Deformation.** On every forward pass through the scene, query coordinates are first passed through TimeNet, which emits a displacement `Δx = vox_motion`; the warped coordinate `x + Δx` is then queried, mapping canonical space to time `t` (`run_network`, `scene_rep.py:194-240`). A **`t = 0` canonical anchor** pins `Δx ≡ 0` at the first frame (`frame_time == 0 → 0`). Setting **`deformation_off`** forces `Δx = 0`, recovering a static Co-SLAM-style model. Crucially, TimeNet receives gradient **only via this warped-coordinate chain rule from the shared render losses** — there is no loss term acting directly on `Δx`.

### Why it was chosen for this thesis

DDS-SLAM is the **closest existing neural-SDF surgical SLAM that already ships a deformation model**, which makes it the natural substrate for this thesis's contributions: adding deformation-aware *uncertainty* and motion *routing* on top of an established canonical-plus-warp formulation rather than building one from scratch. The methodology is **metric-first** — tracking judged by **Sim3-aligned ATE** (up-to-scale depth ⇒ always report scale, est/GT path-ratio, and dominant-axis Pearson alongside it), rendering by **PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS** — with a pristine upstream copy (`DDS-SLAM-Base/`) kept as an eternal reference.

Two findings from our own reproduction must temper any claim that we are standing on a solid headline:

- **The reported StereoMIS advantage does not robustly reproduce.** The paper's bold StereoMIS headline is **~14.8 mm ATE (2.52× over GT motion)** — *not* 8.3 mm, which is a stacked sub-row we previously mis-chased (8.261 mm). In our hands, at **matched configuration**, **DDS-SLAM ≈ Co-SLAM** (the ~18% difference is single-seed and alignment-sensitive, with render tied). The apparent edge largely traces to **generic encoding/config choices** (hash size 16→19, sampling range `n_range_d` 11→16) rather than a DDS-specific innovation. The paper's only direct "field helps" evidence is a single Table III ablation (ATE 9.834→8.261 mm, PSNR 21.906→22.513), which is n=1, sub-SNR, and **capacity-confounded** (removing the warp net also changes parameter count/init).

- **The deformation field is effectively inert as shipped.** TimeNet trains toward bit-exact `Δx = 0`. This is **not a porting bug** — it is faithful to the paper, which specifies five render-only losses (colour, depth, free-space, SDF/truncation, semantic), **none of which act on `Δx`**, and which deliberately forgoes correspondence/flow/motion priors ("eliminating the reliance on explicit data association"). With no positive attractor on `Δx`, the field is **starved by design**. (Config knobs such as `time_smoothness_weight` are unwired K-Planes/HexPlane boilerplate, absent from the paper, and could at most smooth — not revive — a dead field.)

That inertness — a deformation formulation that under-explains motion to zero, the reverse of the published over-explaining collapses — is precisely the gap this thesis targets.

## 3. DDS-SLAM — Tracking Limitations

DDS-SLAM's camera-pose solve is the weakest link in the system on surgical data, and its failures are systematic rather than incidental. The limitations below are grounded in the verified flaws catalog (workflow `wr3dpmcle`, flaws F1–F13), the failure-mode audit (workflow `wzh5qt758`, codes FM1–FM3), and the empirical run logs. Flaw codes (`F#`) and failure-mode codes (`FM#`) are cited at each point.

**1. Sub-SNR jitter — the noise floor *is* the signal (F1).** On low-motion endoscopic sequences the true inter-frame camera motion sits far below the pose-optimiser's intrinsic precision floor. On CRCD C_1/001 the GT active-frame median is 0.115 mm/f against a tracker-stage floor of ~1–2.145 mm/f — i.e. 8.7–18.7× below the optimiser's resolving step. The Adam tracking step (`lr_trans`×`iter` with PyTorch default betas (0.9, 0.999); note the tracker does *not* use (0.9, 0.99) — that is the mapper) produces a per-frame displacement set by optimiser dynamics, not by the visual displacement signal, so the *optimiser's own dynamics dominate the trajectory*. StereoMIS reproduces this independently: GT median 0.20 mm/f (99.9% of frames <1.1 mm), est step ~2.6 mm/f (~13×), and raw ATE grows monotonically 4→62 mm — the fingerprint of random-walk accumulation, not tracking. Critically, this reproduces under the paper's *own* config + depth, so it is intrinsic to the architecture on this regime, not an artefact of our deviations.

**2. `const_speed=True` with no static-frame gate (F2, catastrophic).** `predict_current_pose()` unconditionally extrapolates the previous frame's delta on every new frame, including the 58.2% of CRCD C_1/001 frames where GT is bit-identical (209 of 360 frames < 1 μm motion). During genuine inspect-pauses the tracker has no signal, so it integrates the prior step's noise as pure random-walk drift (≈ √209 × ~1 mm ≈ 14 mm of pseudo-path on an 18.55 mm GT). Static-frame drift is likely the *larger* contributor to path inflation on this snippet. There is no velocity/photometric freeze detector before extrapolation.

**3. lr_trans default is too aggressive.** The default `lr_trans=1e-3` is tuned for cm/frame motion and over-steps sub-mm surgical motion. Dropping both tracking and mapping `lr_trans` 1e-3→1e-4 (the BC0 result, Co-SLAM StereoMIS P2_1, 2026-04-29) cut ATE 39% (70.58→43.12 mm) and path ratio ~6×→2.20×, with the tracker-stage per-frame delta falling 2.145→0.526 mm/f. This is a high-EV magnitude lever, but it fixes step *magnitude* only — direction (Pearson) stayed ~0 across all five BC0 runs. (Demonstrated on Co-SLAM; the equivalent BC0 fix is untested on DDS-SLAM itself.)

**4. No down-weighting of untrustworthy pixels (FM1, catastrophic).** Deforming tissue and the moving instrument enter the photometric pose objective at full weight. Instrument masks are consumed only as Canny edges (`compute_edge_semantic`, `dataset.py`); `valid_depth_mask` (`scene_rep.py`) gates on depth-validity alone, never on semantic class, so moving-tool pixels drive `rgb_loss` and `depth_loss` into the pose solve unweighted. The deformation field provides no relief: on the two CRCD snippets inspected its output is bit-exact Δx=0 — but by *trained convergence to near-zero*, not a hard bug (identity Δx already minimises the render-only losses, and there is no reward for nonzero Δx). The licensable claim is "field inactive on CRCD by trained convergence"; it does **not** license "weights are exactly zero" or "gauge absorption proven" (no CRCD deformation GT exists, and sub-SNR motion is equally consistent with "little real deformation to carry"). Either way, every non-rigid pixel is absorbed by the one rigid pose, corrupting it.

**5. Pearson decoupling (F5).** Across all 9 logged CRCD/StereoMIS runs, |Pearson| between est and GT inter-frame motion stays below 0.2 — far below the sub-SNR theoretical ceiling (≈ √SNR ≈ √0.115 ≈ 0.34 on C_1/001). The tracker shape-tracks at most the dominant-motion axis; the small axes are noise-walked (StereoMIS per-axis Pearson 0.09/0.01/0.08). This is the most direct "tracker isn't tracking" diagnostic, robust to alignment choice, scale, and metric type. Caveat (honesty): at sub-SNR no test yet separates true tracker decoupling from "GT motion simply below resolution" — that requires a cm-scale-motion sequence (e.g. Replica) where post-fix Pearson must rise above 0.5 to prove the fix helps tracking, not just reduces jitter.

**6. Path-length inflation.** Jitter and static-frame drift inflate the estimated trajectory. On SM_v3, est_path was 607 mm against a GT path of 18.55 mm — a 33× ratio, and ~37× larger than passive random-walk integration (√N_total · step ≈ √270 · 1 mm ≈ 16 mm) predicts, indicating the tracker actively *amplifies* noise rather than merely integrating it (depth-scale mis-calibration is the likely amplifier; the ×0.16 depth fix cut est_path from ~2400→607 mm). A genuinely-tracking config requires est/GT path-ratio near 1.0–1.5× and Sim3 scale near 1.0.

**7. Global bundle adjustment is a second random-walker.** Tracking error is not confined to the tracker stage: the mapping / global-BA optimiser re-optimises keyframe poses and walks with its own `lr` (StereoMIS global_BA delta 1.331 mm/f at default). BC0 required dropping `lr_trans` for *both* tracking and mapping — fixing the tracker alone (B0) was insufficient because global_BA then took over as the walker (its delta even rose, 1.331→1.488 mm/f, and ATE went *up* 5%). This confirms keyframe-pose coupling corrupts the per-frame solve and that the two stages must be fixed jointly.

**8. Co-visibility windowing exists but is dead code (FM3).** The live mapping loop calls `sample_global_rays` (all keyframes, no culling) at `ddsslam.py`; the co-visibility cullers `sample_overlap_keyframe` / `sample_global_keyframe` (`keyframe.py`) are defined but never invoked. With no windowing, every keyframe pose stays jointly coupled in global-BA (compounding limitation 7); wiring the existing culler is the lowest-effort FM3 fix.

**9. The evaluation trap — rigid ATE inverts A/Bs (F4, F12).** On up-to-scale monocular (MoGe) depth the est trajectory is ~7.5× the GT metric scale, so rigid (no-scale) Horn ATE (`tools/eval_ate.py`, F12) is dominated by the global scale mismatch and can *invert* A/B verdicts — it reported +uncertainty as +27% **worse**. Conversely, Sim3 (scale-aligned) ATE on sub-SNR data measures cluster *spread*, not trajectory shape (F4): a perfect "hover" scores a *better* Sim3 ATE than a noisy-but-correct tracker, because 7-DOF Horn centres the est cluster on the GT centroid regardless of shape. Tracking quality must therefore be read as a triple — **Sim3 ATE + est/GT path-ratio + dominant-axis |Pearson|** (`Addons/eval/sim3_ate.py`) — never a single number. (Note the paper's own `eval_ate.py` is SE3-only; Sim3 is our monocular workaround and must never be the sole reported figure.)

## 4. BDDS-SLAM — Our Improvements

BDDS-SLAM is our improved model: the pristine DDS-SLAM (a Co-SLAM fork; Wang et al., CVPR 2023) run at an improved configuration — authors' surgical learning rate `lr_trans 1e-4` (the literal `1e-3` in `crcd.yaml` is the catastrophic regime on CRCD), `mapping.cur_frame_iters 100`, and a 64-wide × 3-layer decoder (`hidden_dim 64`, `num_layers 3`, `num_layers_color 3`) — plus four flag-gated feature additions. Every addition is default-off, so a base run is byte-identical to the IRMVLab `009977b` build (parity-gated via `test_inc0_bitidentical.py`: param count + state-dict keys + RNG-after-build). The champion config is `configs/CRCD/crcd_best_rect.yaml` (inheriting `crcd_improved_rect.yaml` → `crcd_basecanon.yaml`). The arbiter throughout is metrics: Sim3 ATE (scale-corrected Umeyama, reported with path-ratio and dominant-axis Pearson — the up-to-scale MoGe depth makes the pipeline's own rigid-Horn ATE scale-dominated and INVERTS the A/B) and render PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS.

### 1. Uncertainty-aware tracking (Inc-1 + Inc-2)
**What.** Inc-1 adds a per-ray aleatoric σ² head — a clone of DDS-SLAM's own `EdgeNet_Semantic` plumbing conditioned on `geo_feat`, with softplus output (floored, fp32 +1e-6) volume-rendered through the existing `raw2outputs`. It is trained **mapping-only** (self-supervised) by the Kendall–Gal heteroscedastic NLL `½(err²/σ² + log σ²)` (`nll_weight 0.1`). Inc-2 then weights the **tracking-only** RGB/depth residual by `clip(1/σ², w_min 0.1, w_max 10).detach()` (`detach_pose_weight: true`), down-weighting high-uncertainty rays in the pose solve only (linear `weights=`, not the rgb·w squaring trick).
**Why.** The NLL is Kendall & Gal (NeurIPS 2017); the σ² head + inverse-variance pose down-weighting is the WildGS-SLAM / NeRF-on-the-go template (Ren et al., CVPR 2024); the surgical per-pixel pose-robustification precedent is Hayoz et al. (IJCARS 2023). Reimplemented natively on the SDF substrate from DDS-SLAM's own `EdgeNet_Semantic` head and `compute_loss(weights=)` primitive — not copied from WildGS (which conditions on 384-d DINO features, not `geo_feat`).
**Metric.** CRCD c1_001, pristine **rectified** base, **n=3** non-overlapping: Sim3 ATE_mean 3.15→**2.43 mm (−23%)**, ATE_max 14.79→**9.43 mm (−36%)**, |Pearson|_dom 0.81→**0.97**; path-ratio ~7.0→~4.1. SemSup render neutral (≤0.3 dB PSNR cost, SSIM gain — the hedge does not cost render). This −23% is the n=3 anchor of the thesis.
**Novelty.** Concede the machinery is standard (textbook aleatoric NLL); own the **substrate + domain transfer** — WildGS-style uncertainty down-weighting ported from in-the-wild Gaussian-splat SLAM to a neural-SDF **surgical, deformable** SLAM substrate to absorb **deformation-induced** tracking spikes, driving both loops. NOT a new uncertainty method.

### 2. Flow-as-sensor camera/scene gate (flow_agree)
**What.** RAFT optical flow (causal, t−8→t; `ref_stride 8`) is fit to a single rigid camera motion via a fundamental matrix (RANSAC); the per-pixel Sampson residual is pooled into unsupervised DINOv2 k-means regions (`n_groups 12`). A region "disagrees" if its median Sampson exceeds a 3-px deadband (`disagree_thresh 0.2`, `cam_thresh 2.0`). We **track only when the scene is camera-dominated** (consensus moving AND regions agree); otherwise we fix the pose. `freeze_ba` re-applies the gate-fixed pose to gate-fixed keyframes **after** global BA, so bundle adjustment cannot wash the gate out via keyframe relative-pose anchoring.
**Why.** Per-pixel σ² is structurally appearance-blind to deformation (tissue slides, colour unchanged, residual ~0 → low σ²). Measuring motion directly via flow + epipolar geometry fixes this. The camera/scene split was validated three ways (`feature_flow_probe.py`, CRCD c1_001 360 fr): synthetic self-test parallax 0.00 vs deform 7.44; GT camera-timing Pearson +0.94 (lag-corrected); ~8× discriminator contrast (scene window mean 5.38 vs camera window 0.64).
**Metric.** CRCD c1_001 (n=1, flow_agree_baf): Sim3 ATE_mean 2.73→**0.82 mm**, ATE_max 12.62→**3.67 mm**, path-ratio **6.90→4.10**, |Pearson|_dom 0.866→**0.990**; gate decisions **91% correct vs GT**; GT-awake camera-pan window −34% ATE; pose-protection at the gallbladder deformation 5.24→**0.34 mm**; render preserved (22.07→22.2). Honest caveat: part of the headline drop is frozen-GT-matching on the ~55%-held still windows; the defensible core is the pan −34% + 91% decision accuracy + the deformation pose-protection. n=1 (n=3 lock pending).
**Novelty.** Label-free dynamic handling — **unsupervised** DINOv2 grouping + geometric epipolar (Sampson) agreement, **no trained segmenter** (differs from WildGS's learned uncertainty MLP and DynaSLAM-class semantic masks; surgical labels are scarce). Closest prior art is NRGS-SLAM (same rigid-vs-deformable contrast + down-weight-deforming-in-tracking, on Gaussian splats) — we differ by SDF substrate, continuous NLL σ², and label-free grouping.

### 3. Deformation-adaptive map optimisation (best_deformiters) — CONDITIONAL claim
**What.** The number of current-frame map iterations scales with the measured deformation residual: `n_iters = cur_frame_iters(100) + deform_iters_scale(200) · moving_frac`, where `moving_frac` comes from the `map_route` **pixel-mode** per-pixel homography reprojection residual `‖p2 − H·p1‖` (`deadband 1.0`, E0-validated and corrected down from the 3.0 that under-routed subtle deformation; `soft_scale 2.0` for proportional routing). Deforming frames get more map compute; static frames get less.
**Why.** A static, time-averaged map smears deforming tissue; adaptive compute spends iterations where motion demands it. This is the mapping-side consumer of the **same motion residual** that gates tracking.
**Metric.** **Pending the 5-snippet rectified benchmark.** On c1_001 it beat plain `best` on render but did **not** beat the uniform more-iters control (`best_curmap200`) — so it is a candidate claim, only confirmed if it clears that control.
**Novelty.** **One motion signal, two consumers** — the same camera/scene residual feeds the tracking down-weight/gate AND the mapping budget; adaptive (not fixed) map compute driven by measured deformation. Conditional on clearing the uniform-iters control.

### 4. Charbonnier robust render loss
**What.** A pseudo-Huber (Charbonnier) RGB loss (`rgb_loss_type charbonnier`, `charbonnier_eps 0.01`) replacing plain L2, down-weighting specular-highlight outliers common in endoscopy.
**Why.** Squared error lets bright speculars dominate the gradient; a robust loss caps their influence.
**Metric.** **+0.15 PSNR / +0.006 SSIM (n=1)** on c1_001.
**Novelty.** Minor; a standard robust loss adapted to specular surgical imagery.

(Note: the champion config also carries `training.map_upweight 2.0` from `best_combine`, but it is **confirmed inert on the SDF substrate** — retained only for fidelity, not claimed.)

### Locked novelty framing (4 claims)
We explicitly **concede** that every component is established — Kendall–Gal NLL, RAFT, epipolar/Sampson geometry, DINOv2 + registers. We **own the combination**, framed as exactly four supervisor-meeting claims:
1. **Substrate + domain transfer** — WildGS-style uncertainty down-weighting ported from Gaussian-splat in-the-wild SLAM to a neural-SDF **surgical deformable** substrate, driving both loops (−23% Sim3 ATE, n=3). NOT a new uncertainty method.
2. **Label-free dynamic handling** — camera-vs-independent-motion split via unsupervised DINOv2 grouping + geometric epipolar agreement, no trained segmenter (differs from WildGS's learned MLP and DynaSLAM-class masks).
3. **One motion signal, two consumers** — the same camera/scene residual feeds the tracking down-weight/gate AND the mapping path; one in-loop sensor across both halves of SLAM (not seen in surgical SLAM).
4. **Deformation-adaptive map optimisation** — scale the *amount* of map optimisation by the measured deformation residual; **CONDITIONAL** on beating both `best` and the uniform-iters control (`best_curmap200`).

Lead with claims 1–3 (in the current BDDS-SLAM build); add claim 4 when `best_deformiters` lands and wins. **Honesty caveats for the writeup:** the −23% is the standalone uncertainty win and is the only **n=3** result — the full combined BDDS-SLAM metrics are still **n=1** (n=3 lock pending); on the flow gate, geo σ² does **not** stack (overlapping problem — the gate already fixes the worst still/scene frames, leaving no jitter headroom). A supervisor will probe novelty; overclaiming the machinery loses the room, so the defensible position is the surgical-SDF-deformable application + label-free design + dual/adaptive use, each carrying a metric.

### Parked: the deformation-field "teacher" (separate FIX branch)
Separately, we revived DDS-SLAM's inert deformation field, which trains to **exactly |Δx| = 0** (proven on real deformation GT via the field-warped pin-EPE judge; cause = **starvation**, weight-decay exonerated — `field_on_wd0` identical |Δx|=0). A self-supervised teacher supplies explicit displacement targets Δx* from DINOv2 correspondence + depth-lift, with the field isolated in its own optimiser (`deform_field_teacher_only`) so the dominant global-BA render loss cannot collapse it first. The held-out pin-EPE judge confirms it **alive: +67% reduction vs +42% for a shuffled-time control, cos > 0** (a hollow, non-zero-but-wrong field would score cos < 0 and HURT). This is real work but is a **FIX-branch direction, deliberately dropped from the headline novelty** (per the locked 4-claim framing); it is parked pending depth-anchoring (the mis-scaled MoGe depth over-warps the metric render band) and the deform-iters benchmark result.

## 5. Benchmarking — Models We Compare Against

### The benchmark protocol

All head-to-head comparison runs on a fixed, rectified **5-snippet CRCD benchmark**: snippets **C1_001, C2_001, E3_005, C3_001, G3_001**. These five are held out from the CRCD segmentation-head training pool (trained on the other 15 of CRCD's 20 snippets). Note the held-out set is not uniformly cross-patient: C1/C2/C3/G3 are true cross-episode folds, whereas **E3_005 shares episode E_3 with the seg-training pool (in-domain)** and is reported separately for seg-head purposes. Every method receives identical inputs and is scored by identical metrics so the comparison is apples-to-apples.

- **Inputs.** Rectified RGB (endoscope barrel distortion removed → pinhole-correct geometry, required because every comparison method is a pinhole camera model with no distortion term; CRCD's `rgb/` frames are pre-rectification/distorted and the `intrinsics.yaml` K is already the *rectified* K) plus rectified intrinsics. Depth is **MoGe-2 monocular depth regenerated on the rectified left frames** (not remapped or reused from the raw-left corpus), made metric by an **every-120-frame stereo anchor** baked into the depth PNGs (`png_depth_scale = 10000`) so training runs at **sc_factor = 1**. A robust anchor gate (per-anchor ratio-spread reject `> 0.50`, cross-anchor consensus reject vs robust median, loud FAIL + `.anchor_quality` marker on too-few inliers) prevents specular/textureless frames from baking a confident-wrong scale (the F_3/007 12.5× failure). Post-hoc Sim3 path-ratio ≈ 1 is the downstream confirmation.
- **Metrics.** Tracking via **Sim3 ATE** (scale-corrected, `Addons/eval/sim3_ate.py` — never the pipeline's rigid-Horn `output.txt`/internal eval) reported with **est/GT path-ratio + dominant-axis Pearson** — CRCD motion is sub-SNR (~18 mm), so ATE alone is misleading; **C2_001 is the only headline-tracking snippet**, the rest lean on render. Rendering via **PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS**, plus **Depth-L1**, plus the canonical **6-panel video**. n=3 seeds is the standing convention where compute allows.
- **The two arms.** The head-to-head is **base DDS-SLAM @ improved config** (pristine model, curmap100 = `cur_frame_iters` 100 + decoder64/3 render levers) vs **our BDDS-SLAM champion**. The external comparison models below sit alongside these two arms. (DDS-SLAM CRCD render yardstick ≈ 26.5 PSNR, user-confirmed ground-truth.)

### Comparison models (ARM-4 benchmark targets)

Three external methods are being onboarded onto the 5-snippet CRCD benchmark, build order **SGS < SNI < SemGauss** (least → most work), all camera-SLAM + semantic-map methods (a coherent regime):

- **SGS-SLAM** — semantic **3D Gaussian Splatting** SLAM. Relevant as a pinhole-3DGS twin of DDS-SLAM and the easiest port. **Status: DONE — 5/5 PASS on A100-80GB.** Pattern: mediocre render on CRCD (PSNR 17.5–22.5), strong tracking on short/medium snippets (C1/C2 Pearson 0.91/0.94) but drifts on long ones (C3/G3 path-ratio 12–13× = scale-inflated; E3 weak track, Pearson 0.25). Uses GT 4-class masks {bg, Liver, Gallbladder, Tool} — **no learned seg head**. Phase-A Replica room0 validated (PSNR ~34–40, ATE RMSE ~0.3 cm, mIoU ~0.90–0.95, matching/beating paper) before CRCD onboarding. (Headline C1_001: Sim3-ATE 3.61 mm / Pearson 0.913 / PSNR 22.45 / SSIM 0.80 / LPIPS 0.315 / Depth-L1 23.5.)
- **SNI-SLAM** — semantic **neural-SDF** (tri-plane feature-field) SLAM. The closest architectural neighbour to DDS-SLAM (both NeRF-SDF), so it isolates representation effects. **Status: characterized at DINOv2 features + GT seg (the locked entry).** Verdict: render-weak on CRCD (c1_001 PSNR ~12.8 / SSIM ~0.61 — **architecturally limited**: attempted cheap fixes (normalize compositing, widen bound) *backfired* (12.8 → 5.0, dark blobs → white blobs), proving the blobs are genuinely empty rays from a mushy tri-plane SDF under depth-pinned narrow-band sampling on noisy monocular depth — an honest finding, not an eval artifact) but a **legitimate tracker** (Sim3-ATE ~5.6 mm, Pearson 0.79, path-ratio 11×; `lr_T/R` 1e-4 is a metric-proven required CRCD change vs the native 1e-3). DINOv3-predicted-seg (run B) was tried and **dropped (negative — degraded the render vs GT-seg)**; SNI stays at DINOv2 features + GT seg. SNI's 8-scene Replica reproduction (ATE Mean 0.825 cm, ~2.08× paper) validated the pipeline upstream. **Open: complete the 5-snippet SNI tracking entry (c2/c3/e3/g3) vs the current c1-only characterization.**
- **SemGauss-SLAM** — semantic **3D Gaussian Splatting** SLAM (per-Gaussian DINO features + classifier). Last in build order. **Status: pending.** Uses the shared **in-domain DINOv2-ViT-B/14** seg head — SNI and SemGauss digest the *same* `DINO2SEG` 16-d compressed feature (768→16), so one shared `dinov2_crcd.pth` loads both (SNI strict=False, SemGauss strict=True). The seg head is deployed **in-domain** for the benchmark (the baselines' own convention; the held-out cross-episode head scores only ~0.34 mIoU vs ~0.93 in-domain — a clean citable side-finding, decoupled from the deploy head).

(Note: **Semantic-SuPer** was scoped as ARM-4 method #4 but **dropped from CRCD** — it is a deformable surfel/ED-graph tracker whose native metric is 2D pixel reprojection error, unmeasurable on CRCD (no IR tattoos/pins) and assuming a static camera (CRCD moves). It remains a **Phase-A reproduction only** — and even that was *not faithful*: the smoke run scored 43.7 px vs the paper's 7.5 px because it used a DDS ResNet-18 depth re-train, not the authors' ResNet-50 Monodepth2 ckpt; pipeline validated end-to-end, number not a reproduction.)

### Gaussian-splatting migration / contingency candidates (NOT current benchmark entries)

A separate **GS-migration family** is staged as a contingency: if the NeRF-SDF BDDS-SLAM underperforms, swap the volume-render backend for 3D Gaussian Splatting. These are **donor stacks, not benchmark competitors**:

- **EndoGSLAM** (MICCAI 2024, surgical RGB-D GS-SLAM loop — true online unknown-pose, photometric+depth tracking ≈ DDS-SLAM's `tracking_render`) = the prospective new eternal base. **Phase-0 verified GO-with-conditions**; unmodified-base seed-0 prelim (n=1) Sim3 ATE **4.21 mm** / Pearson 0.85 (vs SGS 3.31 / NeRF ~3.15) but path-ratio 93× = high pose jitter → headroom for the ported Inc-2 down-weight. Characterized live: tracks cleaner than NeRF (less jitter) but **renders less detail** (CRCD PSNR ~20.4 SH-0, vs SGS 22.6, vs DDS-SLAM ~26.5 — densification is off, coverage-starved) and **does not adapt to scene change** (tool moves → static map ghosts) — the latter is the contribution's problem statement, not a base disqualifier.
- **WildGS-SLAM** — image-space σ² uncertainty; lift the `dyn_uncertainty` logic only (no rasterizer change) for our Inc-1/Inc-2.
- **Deform3DGS** — per-Gaussian RBF deformation basis (applied in pure Python before a stock rasterizer; no custom CUDA kernel needed for Phase-2).
- **SemGauss-SLAM** — also the rasterizer/semantics donor (RGB+depth+sem channel).

Net-new contribution is **NOT** "learned rigid/deform routing" (NRGS-SLAM, arXiv:2602.17182, Feb 2026, already routes per-Gaussian rigid/deform with a Bayesian weight — concede it). The headline is **per-Gaussian RBF-basis deformation streamed online + object-level tool SE(3) lifetimes** (unclaimed by NRGS and the 2025–26 surgical-GS literature). GS is a 4–8-week post-deadline direction; Phase-1 (replicate the −23%/−36% ATE uncertainty win in GS form) is the guaranteed-shippable milestone, Phase-2 online-deformable is the open research (no released online-deformable surgical GS exists).

### Substrate / regression references

**Co-SLAM** is the upstream substrate DDS-SLAM forks (not a CRCD entry); the **vanilla Co-SLAM Replica office0 = 5.87 mm** result (Pearson 0.688, inflation 1.04×) is the environment-validation/regression gate (no published Co-SLAM Replica ATE exists — this is a healthy-tracker fingerprint, not a paper-match), and the **SNI-SLAM 8-scene Replica reproduction** (ATE Mean 0.825 cm, RMSE 1.310 cm, Comp.Ratio 90.07% — ~2.08× paper ATE, single-seed) validated the SNI pipeline before its CRCD onboarding.
